## Overview

A home battery is rarely alone: it shares a grid connection with pv panels and sometimes a heat pump.
That is why the battery API has two levels:

* **Households**: a house and all the NOX-connected devices in it, behind its grid connection. It is read-only.
* **Batteries**: the individual devices, with their capabilities, schedules and settings.

A household adds no identity to its devices: the `device_id`s it lists are the same ones the
[heat pump](/guides/hp-api-guide), [pv](/guides/pv-api-guide) and battery endpoints return.

The **NOX Control Package** lets NOX Energy optimize your users' batteries and provides access to:
* Battery information: capacity, maximum charge and discharge power, discharge limit and supported modes
* Battery data
    * Current telemetry: state of charge, power and current mode
    * Measured charge and discharge (15 min)
    * Typical charge and discharge: how the battery would have behaved without steering
    * Forecasted charge and discharge planned by NOX Energy, to use in your nominations
* Household data: current house load and grid power, and the house load forecast
* Changing battery settings

The **Partner Control Package** lets you steer the batteries yourself. It provides access to the same battery
information, battery data (except the NOX forecast, as NOX Energy does not plan these batteries) and household
data, and additionally to:
* Steering batteries by submitting battery schedules

## Getting Started

### Prerequisites
* Valid NOX API key with access to the battery features (see [Authentication](/api-docs/authentication))

### Base URL
All endpoints use the base URL:
- Sandbox: `https://api.sandbox.nox.energy`
- Production: `https://api.nox.energy`

## Core Workflows

<Note>
    All our collection api endpoints use a `next_token` to paginate through results if the size limit of the response has been reached.
    To paginate through a response, you can use the `next_token` received in the response as a query parameter and call the same endpoint with
    the same parameters again. You can repeat this process until you have received the last page of the response, which is signified
    by the `next_token` field becoming `null`.
    Pass the `next_token` back exactly as you received it. A token that was altered or not issued by the API returns a `400`.
</Note>

### 1. Getting households and batteries

Fetch the household of a user, then their batteries:

<CodeGroup dropdown>

```bash cURL
curl --request GET \
  --url 'https://api.nox.energy/v1/households?user_id=1234' \
  --header 'x-api-key: <api-key>'

curl --request GET \
  --url 'https://api.nox.energy/v1/devices/battery?user_id=1234' \
  --header 'x-api-key: <api-key>'
```

```python Python
import requests

headers = {"x-api-key": "<api-key>"}

households = requests.get("https://api.nox.energy/v1/households",
                          params={"user_id": "1234"}, headers=headers).json()

batteries = requests.get("https://api.nox.energy/v1/devices/battery",
                         params={"user_id": "1234"}, headers=headers).json()
```

</CodeGroup>

How the devices are wired (for example whether a pv installation and a battery share one inverter) is handled
by NOX Energy and is not exposed. It is reflected in each battery's `allowed_modes`, together with the battery model.

### Power sign convention

All power values follow one rule: **positive means power consumed, negative means power delivered**.

| Field | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Battery `power_kw` | Charging | Discharging |
| Household `grid_power_kw` | Importing from the grid | Exporting to the grid |
| Household `house_load_kw` | Consumption (always positive) | |

### 2. Battery modes

You steer a battery by asking it to follow a **battery mode** during a time window:

| Mode | Behaviour | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `auto` (default) | The battery follows its own logic: pv surplus charges it, it covers the house load, the grid covers the rest. | Most hours; the battery returns to it outside your schedules and whenever NOX takes over. |
| `pause_charge` | The battery does not charge; pv surplus is exported. It can still discharge. | Keep headroom for negative-price hours later; export while injection prices are high. |
| `pause_discharge` | The battery does not discharge; the grid covers the house load. Pv can still charge it. | Cheap import now, keep the stored energy for the evening peak. |
| `force_charge` | Charge at the battery's fixed charge power, from pv first and the grid for the rest. | Negative or very low prices. |
| `force_discharge` | Discharge at the battery's fixed discharge power, to the house first and the grid for the rest, while the state of charge is above `min_soc_pct`. | Price spikes. |

Only modes listed in the battery's `allowed_modes` are accepted.

`min_soc_pct` (fixed at 20 % for now) is the lower limit for `force_discharge`. When the state of charge reaches it,
NOX Energy takes control and puts the battery in `auto` for the rest of the schedule entry. It does not limit `auto`
itself, where the battery may keep covering the house load.

### 3. Planning your own battery schedule (Partner Control Package)

To steer a battery yourself, you first need to know what the household will need. Two forecasts give you that,
both per 15 minutes in kWh:

* **House load forecast** with [GET /households/house-load/forecast](/api-docs/household/asset-info/get-households-house-load-forecast):
  what the household will consume, without its battery and before pv production is subtracted. Heat pump
  consumption is included.
* **Pv production forecast** with [GET /devices/pv/production/forecast](/api-docs/pv/asset-info/get-devices-pv-production-forecast):
  what the pv installation of the household will produce. This requires access to the pv forecast
  (see the [PV API Guide](/guides/pv-api-guide)).

Subtract the pv production from the house load, quarter by quarter, to get the household's **net load**:

* **Net load positive**: the household needs energy. The battery can cover it (`auto`) or keep its energy for a
  more expensive moment (`pause_discharge`).
* **Net load negative**: the household has a pv surplus. The battery can store it (`auto`) or leave room for a
  cheaper moment later (`pause_charge`).

Combine the net load with your price signals (day-ahead prices, your tariffs, imbalance prices) and the battery's
limits to run your own optimization:

* the current state of charge from [GET /devices/battery/telemetry/current](/api-docs/battery/asset-info/get-devices-battery-telemetry-current),
* `capacity_kwh`, `max_charge_power_kw`, `max_discharge_power_kw`, `min_soc_pct` and `allowed_modes` from
  [GET /devices/battery](/api-docs/battery/asset-info/get-devices-battery).

The result is a list of time windows with the best mode for each, which you send in the next step.

<CodeGroup dropdown>

```python Python
import requests

headers = {"x-api-key": "<api-key>"}
period = {"start_time": "2026-10-17T00:00:00Z", "end_time": "2026-10-18T00:00:00Z"}

house_load = requests.get("https://api.nox.energy/v1/households/house-load/forecast",
                          params={**period, "household_id": "<household_id>"}, headers=headers).json()
pv = requests.get("https://api.nox.energy/v1/devices/pv/production/forecast",
                  params={**period, "device_id": "<pv_device_id>"}, headers=headers).json()

pv_by_quarter = {row["forecast_time"]: float(row["production"]) for row in pv["data"]}
net_load = {row["forecast_time"]: row["house_load_kwh"] - pv_by_quarter.get(row["forecast_time"], 0.0)
            for row in house_load["data"]}
# Feed net_load, your prices and the battery limits into your own optimization.
```

</CodeGroup>

### 4. Submitting schedules (Partner Control Package)

Make sure `flex_trading` is enabled for the battery (see [Settings](#7-settings)), then send one schedule per battery with
[POST /devices/battery/schedules](/api-docs/battery/battery-control/post-battery-control-schedule).
A new schedule for a battery replaces everything previously sent for it. Outside your time windows the battery
falls back to `auto`, and every `start_time` must be at least 1 minute in the future.

```bash cURL
curl --request POST \
  --url 'https://api.nox.energy/v1/devices/battery/schedules' \
  --header 'x-api-key: <api-key>' \
  --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data '[
    {
      "device_id": "e4da3b7f-bbce-4a45-9d2b-1c0e8f7a6b5c",
      "schedules": [
        {"start_time": "2026-10-17T11:00:00Z", "end_time": "2026-10-17T14:00:00Z", "mode": "pause_charge"},
        {"start_time": "2026-10-17T18:00:00Z", "end_time": "2026-10-17T19:30:00Z", "mode": "force_discharge"}
      ]
    }
  ]'
```

A `200` means every schedule was accepted. A `207` lists the rejected batteries with a `reason` and
`reason_code`, for example when the mode is not in `allowed_modes` or `flex_trading` is not enabled for the battery.

### 5. Nominating NOX-optimized batteries (NOX Control Package)

For batteries NOX Energy optimizes, fetch the planned 15 minute charge and discharge with
[GET /devices/battery/forecast/historical](/api-docs/battery/asset-info/get-devices-battery-forecast-historical)
and include it in your nominations, just as you do with the heat pump consumption forecast.
The forecast is made every day at a fixed time depending on supplier.

Add the [house load forecast](/api-docs/household/asset-info/get-households-house-load-forecast) to nominate the
rest of the household's consumption.

### 6. Measuring the value of steering

[GET /devices/battery/typical-charge-discharge/historical](/api-docs/battery/asset-info/get-devices-battery-typical-charge-discharge-historical)
returns a simulated baseline: how each battery would have behaved in `auto` (pure self-consumption), using the realised
pv production and house load. Compare it per 15 minutes with the measured values from
[GET /devices/battery/charge-discharge/historical](/api-docs/battery/asset-info/get-devices-battery-charge-discharge-historical)
to calculate what steering earned.

### 7. Settings

Battery settings are read with [GET /devices/battery/settings](/api-docs/battery/user-management/get-devices-battery-settings)
and changed with [PATCH /devices/battery/settings](/api-docs/battery/user-management/patch-devices-battery-settings).
Provide a `device_id` to change one battery, or a `user_id` to change all batteries of a user.

Each battery has four on/off switches in `optimization_settings`. Together they decide **who steers the battery**:

* **You steer** (Partner Control Package): turn on `flex_trading`. NOX Energy then follows the schedules you send
  (step 4).
* **NOX Energy steers** (NOX Control Package): turn on one or more of these, they can be combined:
    * `dynamic_tariff`: NOX Energy charges the battery when day-ahead prices in the user's country are low and uses
      the energy when they are high.
    * `pv_self_consumption`: NOX Energy stores surplus solar energy to use it in the house later, instead of
      exporting it.
    * `imbalance_optimization`: NOX Energy also reacts to the imbalance prices of the user's country.
* **Nobody steers**: turn everything off. NOX Energy leaves the battery alone and it stays in `auto`.

You cannot mix both: turning on `flex_trading` turns the three NOX switches off, and turning on any NOX switch
turns `flex_trading` off. If you have the NOX Control Package, use the NOX switches and leave `flex_trading` off.

```bash cURL
curl --request PATCH \
  --url 'https://api.nox.energy/v1/devices/battery/settings' \
  --header 'x-api-key: <api-key>' \
  --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data '{"device_id": "e4da3b7f-bbce-4a45-9d2b-1c0e8f7a6b5c", "optimization_settings": {"dynamic_tariff": true, "imbalance_optimization": true}}'
```
